How Guest Extension Pricing works

When rates change

When a guest sends a Hostaway message that matches an extension request, Stay Automation checks the reservation and the listing’s owner tag. Pricing must be enabled for that owner. If you set a minimum or maximum booked length, those limits apply to the guest’s existing reservation.

Starting on the booked checkout date, the automation can mark up consecutive open or eligible blocked nights, up to seven nights. It stops at the first reserved, pending, or otherwise ineligible night, even when more nights are open beyond it. Calendar-rate discounts apply only to consecutive eligible blocked nights, within the same seven-night limit. It changes rates only. Calendar holds, guest quotes, and checkout are handled separately.

How the nightly rate is calculated

  • Calendar rate. Apply the adjustment to each eligible night’s Hostaway rate. A 10% markup raises a $200 night to $220. A 10% discount lowers it to $180.
  • Booked stay average. Divide the original accommodation charge by the number of booked nights, then add any markup. A $1,500 accommodation charge over 10 nights averages $150. With a 10% markup, the target is $165. A night already priced at $180 stays at $180.

Stay Automation saves the original and adjusted rates for each tracked night, so repeat requests or overnight rewrites use the saved rate and avoid compounding the adjustment.

What happens overnight

For {{ owner }}, saving starts at {{ schedule.save }} Eastern, before the 2:00 a.m. PriceLabs sync. Pricing writes pause from the sync until {{ schedule.restore }} Eastern, when Stay Automation starts rewriting saved rates for nights that are still eligible. When a tracked night becomes reserved, pending, or otherwise ineligible, Stay Automation stops rewriting that night and later nights in the same extension.

How to read the booking report

A night earns credit when the requesting guest books it while a verified markup is in effect, before the next 2:00 a.m. Eastern PriceLabs sync. Hostaway must match the original reservation or the same guest on the same booking channel.

  • Rent at saved rate is the saved nightly rate for that booked night.
  • Increase at stay average shows how far the original stay average was above the original calendar rate, before the markup, when an average was recorded.
  • Net increase after markup is the saved nightly rate minus the original calendar rate.

For example, if the original calendar rate is $140, the stay average is $150, and the final rate is $165, the report shows $165 in rent at the saved rate, a $10 increase at the stay average, and a $25 net increase after markup. The figures use saved rates and are grouped by currency.
